# Business Opportunities in Azerbaijan, 2025–2030
### A data-driven, source-verified market opportunity report

**Prepared:** 18 July 2026
**Method:** Multi-angle deep research across 25 fetched sources (IMF, EBRD, World Bank, US State Department, US Commercial Service, Azerbaijan State Statistical Committee, presidential decrees, industry press). 55 falsifiable claims were extracted; the top 25 were each adversarially verified by a 3-vote fact-check panel. **23 claims confirmed, 2 refuted.** Every figure below carries a confidence label: **[Verified]** (3-0 panel confirmation against primary sources) or **[Reported]** (extracted from a credible secondary source but not put through the verification panel).

---

## 1. Executive summary

Azerbaijan's best business opportunities for 2025–2030 lie almost entirely in the **non-oil economy** — and this is not an analyst's guess, it is the state's own declared policy. The "Azerbaijan 2030" national strategy (presidential order, 2 Feb 2021) explicitly repositions the non-oil economy as "the center of development" and designates the private sector, especially SMEs, as the intended main source of growth and employment. **[Verified]**

The verified 2024 data identify four sectors growing **2–4x faster than the overall economy** (GDP +4.1%, non-oil GDP +6.1–6.2%):

| Rank | Sector | Key verified 2024 datapoint | Growth signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **ICT / digital services** | Revenue AZN 3.65bn (~US$2.15bn), **+13.2% y/y**; software dev +31% | Fastest verified revenue growth; low capital intensity |
| 2 | **Tourism & hospitality** | 2.63m foreign visitors, **+25.9% y/y**; still ~17.5% below 2019 peak | Fastest verified demand growth, with recovery headroom |
| 3 | **Construction / real estate (incl. Karabakh reconstruction)** | Led 6.2% non-oil growth (IMF); ~US$2.35bn/yr state reconstruction budget | Fiscally anchored demand via "Great Return" national priority |
| 4 | **Transport & logistics (Middle Corridor)** | IMF-named 2024 growth driver; EBRD investment focus | Geopolitical upside from Aug-2025 Azerbaijan–Armenia declaration |
| 5 | **Renewable energy** | National target: ≥30% renewables in installed capacity by 2030 | Strongest policy + institutional-capital tailwind; capex-heavy |
| 6 | **Agribusiness / manufacturing / services SME niches** | Named EBRD 2025–2030 financing targets | DFI-financeable, but thinner verified growth data |
| — | *Watchlist: e-commerce & fintech* | Cashless payments ~US$5.15bn in Jul-2025, +20.2% y/y [Reported] | Strong fundamentals; market-size data not yet verifiable |

Entry economics are sweetened by the **Alat Free Economic Zone** (full tax and customs exemption, its own regulatory and arbitration regime, nationalization protection, free capital flows) **[Verified]** and by the **EBRD's 2025–2030 country strategy** (>€3.6bn invested to date; SMEs, ICT, Middle Corridor transport and renewables named as financing destinations). **[Verified]**

The core caution: growth is **sector-specific, not macro-broad**. Headline GDP growth collapsed to +1.0% y/y in Jan–Aug 2025, and the economy remains structurally hydrocarbon-dependent (~85–97% of export revenue, roughly half the state budget). Buy sector exposure, not country beta. **[Verified]**

## 2. Macroeconomic context and trends

### 2.1 Growth: real, but decelerating and uneven

- **2024:** Real GDP **+4.1%** (up from +1.4% in 2023), driven by **non-hydrocarbon GDP +6.2%** (IMF; EBRD reports 6.1% on a slightly different definition), concentrated in **construction, communications/ICT, transportation, trade, tourism and hospitality**. Nominal 2024 GDP: AZN 126.3bn (State Statistical Committee). **[Verified]**
- **Non-oil share:** more than **two-thirds of output** (EBRD); the Economy Minister cites **71.5%** for 2025. **[Verified]**
- **2025 slowdown:** real GDP **+1.0% y/y in Jan–Aug 2025** — non-oil +2.6%, hydrocarbons −2.1%. EBRD forecast 2.0% for 2025 and 2.5% for 2026 (a downgrade from 3.0%); its June-2026 update puts actual 2025 growth at **1.4%** and cuts 2026 to **2.0%**. Fundamentals judged sound on low public debt and ample FX reserves. **[Verified]**
- **Inflation:** contained at **4.9%** at end-2024, inside the central bank's 4±2% band. **[Reported — IMF Art. IV]**
- **2026 outlook:** government forecast of AZN 134.1bn nominal GDP closely aligns with the IMF's AZN 132.5bn (~US$77.9bn, real growth ~2.1%) — a ~1.2% gap. Nominal **non-oil** GDP is projected at AZN 100.7bn in 2026, ~+9.1% y/y nominal — roughly three-quarters of nominal GDP. **[Reported]**

**Investor read:** the non-oil economy is outgrowing the headline economy by 2–4x. The 2025 slowdown argues for **sector selection over macro exposure**.

### 2.2 The policy framework: "Azerbaijan 2030"

The decade-defining strategy — *"Azerbaijan 2030: National Priorities for Socio-Economic Development"* (presidential order, 2 Feb 2021) — sets exactly **five national priorities**: **[Verified]**

1. A steadily growing, competitive economy
2. A dynamic, inclusive society based on social justice
3. Competitive human capital and a space for modern innovations
4. The **"Great Return"** to the liberated (Karabakh / Eastern Zangezur) territories
5. A clean environment and "green growth"

Verbatim, the order states that *"the non-oil economy should be harnessed as the center of development"* and that for *"the private sector, especially small- and medium-sized businesses, to become the main source of economic growth and employment"* the government must *"significantly improve business environments… increase the transparency of the judicial system, and… prevent unfair competition."* **[Verified — president.az, corroborated by UNECE, IEA, World Bank and ADB documents; ADB's 2025–2029 Country Partnership Strategy still cites it as current]**

Supporting policy signals:
- **March 2025:** presidential decree established an **AI Strategy 2025–2028**; an AI Academy opened in Baku partnering with MIT, Stanford and Carnegie Mellon. **[Reported]**
- **2024 reforms:** new competition law enacted, business licensing simplified, first renewable-energy auction concluded. **[Reported — US State Dept 2025 Investment Climate Statement]**
- **E-government:** Azerbaijan entered the UN E-Government Development Index "Very High" category for the first time. **[Reported]**

### 2.3 Institutional capital: the EBRD 2025–2030 country strategy

Approved 29 Jan 2025 with exactly two priorities: (1) economic diversification via a stronger private sector and improved governance; (2) accelerating the green transition and regional connectivity. It **names its financing destinations**: SME investment in agribusiness, manufacturing and services, ICT, and infrastructure; sustainable transport, municipal infrastructure and digital connectivity **tied to the Middle Corridor**; solar/wind, grid strengthening and green-electricity exports backing the national **≥30%-renewables-by-2030** capacity target. Cumulative EBRD investment: **over €3.6bn across ~200 projects**. **[Verified]**

Aligning a market entry with these named sectors materially de-risks financing — a significant advantage given the weaknesses in local bank lending (Section 5.2).

## 3. Ranked opportunities

Ranking triangulates three verified dimensions: **measured 2024 growth**, **policy-priority alignment**, and **institutional-capital signals**. The rank ordering itself is analytic judgment on top of verified data (labeled where each datapoint stands).

### #1 — ICT and digital services

- **Market size / growth:** ICT services revenue **AZN 3.65bn (~US$2.15bn) in 2024, +13.2% y/y** — with **software development +31%** and **internet services +29%**. ICT is ~1.8–1.9% of GDP, implying large diversification headroom. **[Verified — US Commercial Service / State Statistical Committee]**
- **Sub-sector detail [Reported]:** mobile telephony is the largest single subsector (AZN 1.24bn, +6.7%); wireless telecom services +29%; other ICT services +19%.
- **Demand fundamentals:** **89% internet penetration** (9.23m users), 12.2m mobile connections (118% of population — SIM counts, not unique users), 6.73m social-media identities (64.9% of residents), as of 2025. **[Verified — DataReportal Digital 2025]**
- **Why it ranks first:** fastest verified *revenue* growth of any sector; explicitly state-priority (digitalization strategy, AI strategy); a named EBRD financing target; **low capital intensity** and the shortest path to revenue for a new entrant.
- **Entry angles:** software development and outsourcing services, e-government/enterprise digitization contracting, AI/data services (riding the 2025–2028 AI strategy), telecom-adjacent infrastructure.

### #2 — Tourism and hospitality

- **Demand growth:** **2,626.7 thousand (~2.63m) foreign and stateless visitors from 196 countries in 2024, +25.9% y/y** — the fastest verified demand growth of any sector. Hospitality is IMF-named among 2024's leading non-oil growth sectors. **[Verified — State Statistical Committee / State Border Service]**
- **Headroom:** arrivals remained roughly **17.5% below the 2019 pre-pandemic peak** — this is recovery-phase growth with room left. **[Verified]** (Note: ~1.86m of the 2.63m arrived specifically for tourism purposes — "visitors" is the precise framing.)
- **Source-market mix [Reported]:** concentrated — Russia 27.8%, Türkiye 16.2%; **India (9.3%) has emerged as the #3 source market**, signaling new South Asia / Gulf demand segments. Outbound travel by Azerbaijani citizens also grew +15.5% in 2024 (2.16m trips), a domestic-demand indicator for travel services; 69.4% of outbound trips go by air.
- **Entry angles:** mid-market hotel and serviced-apartment supply, inbound tour operations targeting India/Gulf segments, hospitality tech and services, MICE/events.
- **Risk to price in:** source-market concentration (Russia + Türkiye = 44% of arrivals).

### #3 — Construction and real estate, incl. Karabakh reconstruction

- **Growth:** construction **led** the 6.2% non-oil expansion in 2024 (IMF names it first among drivers). **[Verified]**
- **Fiscal anchor:** reconstruction of Karabakh / Eastern Zangezur is national priority #4 ("Great Return"). The 2025 state budget allocates **AZN 4bn (~US$2.35bn)** for reconstruction; cumulative allocations since 2020 reached **~AZN 17.5bn (~US$10.3bn) by Nov 2024**, and roughly **US$13–13.5bn by late 2025**, implying continued annual allocations of AZN 3–4.5bn through 2030. **[Reported — Caspian News / parliamentary budget committee; not panel-verified]**
- **The caveat that matters:** the IMF recommends **fiscal consolidation of 1.5–2pp of non-oil GDP annually for ~10 years** (the non-oil primary deficit was 20.5% of non-oil GDP in 2024). A decade of tightening could dampen state-driven construction demand in the out-years. **[Reported — IMF Art. IV]**
- **Entry angles:** subcontracting into reconstruction programs (materials, engineering services, utilities), Baku commercial/residential development, building-materials manufacturing (import substitution).
- **Open question:** tender openness to foreign contractors is not well documented — see Section 7.

### #4 — Transport and logistics on the Middle Corridor

- **Growth:** transportation is IMF-named among 2024's main non-oil growth drivers. **[Verified]**
- **Institutional capital:** EBRD explicitly commits to sustainable transport, municipal infrastructure and digital connectivity **"with a particular focus on projects linked to the Middle Corridor."** **[Verified]**
- **Geopolitical upside:** on **8 August 2025**, Azerbaijan and Armenia signed a US-mediated joint declaration aiming to establish a new unimpeded regional connectivity route through the South Caucasus. Per the EBRD: *"If this project materialises, it will open a new avenue for east-west trade and transit traffic through Azerbaijan."* A US–Armenia implementation framework (the TRIPP route) followed in January 2026. **The peace treaty remains initialed but unsigned — this upside is prospective, not booked.** **[Verified]**
- **Entry angles:** freight forwarding and 3PL along the Trans-Caspian route, port/rail-adjacent warehousing (especially at Alat — see Section 4), customs brokerage and trade-tech, cold chain for agri-exports.
- **Synergy:** this is the sector where the Alat FEZ incentive stack is strongest, since the zone sits next to the Port of Alat.

### #5 — Renewable energy (solar, wind, grid, green exports)

- **Policy anchor:** national target of **at least 30% renewables in installed electricity capacity by 2030**, backed by the EBRD with solar/wind, grid-strengthening and green-electricity-export support; first renewables auction completed in 2024. **[Verified / Reported]**
- **Why #5 despite the strongest tailwind:** project cycles are long and capex-heavy, revenue is contracted rather than market-growth-driven, and utility-scale deals concentrate among large sponsors (e.g., Gulf developers) — a harder space for new mid-size entrants than ICT or tourism.
- **Entry angles:** EPC and O&M services for utility-scale projects, distributed/commercial solar, grid equipment and services, green-hydrogen/energy-export ancillary services.

### #6 — Agribusiness, manufacturing and services SME niches

- **Signal:** these are **named EBRD SME financing targets** for 2025–2030 ("agribusiness, manufacturing and services"). **[Verified]** Verified sector-level growth data are thinner than for sectors 1–4 (the EBRD's sector list is introduced with "such as" — illustrative, not exhaustive).
- **Entry angles:** export-oriented agri-processing (leveraging Middle Corridor logistics), light manufacturing for import substitution, B2B services. DFI co-financing is the realistic funding path given local credit constraints.

### Watchlist — e-commerce and fintech (fundamentals strong, market sizing unverified)

No market-size claims for e-commerce or fintech survived source-quality screening, so they are not ranked. But the reported indicators are notable **[all Reported — Fintech Times, CBA strategy documents]**:

- Total cashless payments reached **~US$5.15bn in July 2025, +20.2% y/y**, with **>87% of transactions via e-commerce channels**.
- Fintech companies operating in Azerbaijan grew from ~22 (late 2022) to an estimated **55 by 2026** — roughly doubling in three years. Kapital Bank's Birbank super-app passed **3.4m active users**.
- The Central Bank's **Financial Sector Development Strategy 2024–2026** prioritizes open banking, digital payments and financial inclusion, with CBDC exploration and an API-based open-finance ecosystem; targets include cashless >40% of retail transactions.
- The verified demand-side fundamentals from #1 (89% internet penetration, 118% mobile) directly support both segments.

**Read:** likely a genuine growth market, entering the regulatory-enablement phase — but do independent market-size diligence before committing capital.

## 4. Ease of doing business, incentives and market entry

### 4.1 Alat Free Economic Zone (AFEZ) — the strongest verified instrument

Located next to the Port of Alat, ~50 miles south of Baku, on the Middle Corridor. Under the 2018 AFEZ law: **[Verified — statute + US State Dept 2025 ICS]**

- **Full exemption from taxes and customs** for all resident businesses
- The zone administration sets its **own employment, migration, dispute-resolution and arbitration regulations** (a parallel regulatory regime)
- **Protection against nationalization**
- **Guaranteed free flow of funds** in and out of the zone

Two verified qualifications: 2022 amendments narrowed the exclusive jurisdiction of the zone's dispute-resolution bodies, and goods sold **from** AFEZ **into** mainland Azerbaijan face customs duties unless a conditional 15-year exemption applies (requires ≥50% of output exported; effective 1 Jan 2027). The blanket exemption covers activity within and through the zone — i.e., AFEZ is optimized for **export/transit plays, not domestic-market plays**.

### 4.2 General regime and reforms

- Baseline taxes: 20% corporate income tax, 18% VAT, with investment-certificate exemptions available. **[Reported — EY Doing Business in Azerbaijan 2025]**
- 2024: new competition law, simplified business licensing, first renewables auction. **[Reported]**
- E-government maturity (ASAN service centers, digital ID, UN EGDI "Very High") lowers administrative friction for registration and compliance. **[Reported]**

### 4.3 Financing reality

Local bank credit is constrained (Section 5.2). The practical financing paths for entrants are: **DFI alignment** (EBRD named sectors, ADB), **export-credit-agency backing**, or offshore equity — not local debt.

## 5. Risks and barriers

### Risk 1 — Hydrocarbon dependence (the macro transmission channel) **[Verified]**

Oil and gas dominate export revenues — EBRD cites ~96.9% on a multi-decade framing; 2024 customs actuals are ~85–90% — and roughly **half the state budget** (~48% in 2024–2025). The IMF's April-2025 FSAP is explicit: reliance on hydrocarbon revenues *"renders Azerbaijan vulnerable to oil and gas price declines. This in turn would impact banks' asset quality and liquidity."* The same IMF assessment found the financial system "broadly resilient against severe shocks," though solvency stress tests showed some thinly-capitalized banks would breach regulatory minimums in the adverse scenario. An oil-price slump therefore transmits to: state spending (including reconstruction budgets), bank credit supply, and consumer demand.

### Risk 2 — The financing environment **[Verified]**

The IMF identifies three structural weaknesses in the banking system:
1. Reliance on **volatile wholesale corporate deposits**
2. **High dollarization** — FX deposits 39% of total, FX business loans 31% (July 2024), often to unhedged borrowers (CBAR 2025 data show continued de-dollarization to ~35.6%/~14.2%, so easing but elevated)
3. **High credit concentration** — top-five exposures equal to **63% of aggregate Tier 1 capital**

Practical implication: do not build a business plan around local debt financing.

### Risk 3 — Business-climate barriers **[Verified]**

The EBRD names three: **deficiencies in the legal-judicial system, high customs tariffs, and a large SOE presence** — including politically connected conglomerates with dominant market positions (*"While SOEs have not been officially granted monopolistic powers, many benefit from a dominant market position"*). Corroborated by the US State Department (judicial independence concerns; a small group of government-connected holding companies dominating the economy; reported expropriation instances, notably in real estate) and by ADB, World Bank and OECD reviews. SOEs account for roughly **40% of GDP** **[Reported — State Dept]**.

Mitigations: operate via **Alat FEZ** where the model allows; contract under **offshore arbitration**; avoid head-on competition in SOE-adjacent sectors; partner with established local groups where market access requires it.

### Risk 4 — Fiscal consolidation and the 2025 slowdown **[Verified / Reported]**

Growth fell to +1.0% y/y (Jan–Aug 2025; full-year 1.4%). The IMF's recommended decade-long fiscal consolidation would progressively squeeze state-driven demand — most relevant to construction (#3). Energy-price volatility and regional geopolitics remain the EBRD's named downside risks, with global trade uncertainty third.

## 6. Recommendations by entrant profile

**For a services entrepreneur / SME (fastest path to revenue):**
Enter **ICT/digital services (#1)** or **tourism services (#2)**. Both have double-digit verified growth, low capital intensity, and direct policy support. In ICT, target software development (+31% in 2024) and AI/e-government work riding the 2025–2028 AI strategy. In tourism, build for the fastest-growing source segments (India, Gulf) rather than the saturated Russia/Türkiye base.

**For an industrial / logistics investor:**
Anchor at **Alat FEZ** for Middle Corridor logistics or export manufacturing (#4, #6) — the tax/customs/arbitration stack is the strongest verified incentive in the country, and the location sits on the corridor's port. Structure financing with the EBRD's named priorities (agribusiness, manufacturing, transport connectivity) to access DFI capital. Treat the Armenia-corridor upside as an option, not a base case.

**For a construction / materials player:**
The **Karabakh reconstruction program (#3)** offers fiscally anchored demand (~US$2.35bn/yr), but diligence tender access for foreign firms first (an open question — Section 7), and model out-year revenue against the IMF's consolidation path. Building-materials import substitution hedges the tender-access risk.

**For an energy investor:**
**Renewables (#5)** has the clearest 2030 policy target and EBRD backing, but expect long cycles and large-sponsor competition at utility scale. Mid-size entrants do better in EPC/O&M, distributed solar, and grid services than in project ownership.

**For everyone:**
- Do not rely on local bank debt (Risk 2). Use DFI, ECA or offshore funding.
- Put dispute resolution offshore or inside AFEZ's regime (Risk 3).
- Stress-test every plan against a sustained oil-price decline (Risk 1) — it hits state spending, credit supply and FX simultaneously.
- Prefer sector exposure over macro exposure: the 2025 numbers show the headline economy can stall while target sectors keep compounding.

## 7. Data gaps and open questions

These matter for diligence and were **not** resolvable from verified sources:

1. **FDI inflows** (2023–2026 levels and trends, especially non-oil FDI) — no claim survived verification.
2. **Karabakh reconstruction access model** — verified budget totals exist only via secondary press; tender openness to foreign contractors is undocumented.
3. **E-commerce / fintech / agritech market sizes** — ranked here on demand-side proxies and reported indicators only.
4. **Current IMF medium-term projections** — the specific figures circulating (3.5% for 2025, ~2.5% medium-term, −2.0% oil GDP in 2026) were **refuted** in verification; obtain the latest IMF Article IV directly.

## 8. Claims that failed verification (do not cite)

1. *"The government has designated exactly four priority non-oil sectors (agriculture, tourism, ICT, transportation/logistics)."* — Refuted 0–3. The actual framework is the five-priority Azerbaijan 2030 order; the four-sector list is a mischaracterization.
